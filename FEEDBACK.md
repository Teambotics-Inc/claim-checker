# How feedback works here

Claim Checker audits other people's claims. The fastest way to keep us honest is to make our own work maximally challengeable. This page is the contract for that.

## The loop

1. **An audit is published** with its evidence, verdicts, and limits stated on its face — and, before publication, its subject was given the verbatim claims and draft verdicts and a **14-day window** to respond (see the Standard's *Before publication: right of reply*). The response, or the fact of non-response, is part of the audit.
2. **Anyone can challenge it** — through the "Challenge an audit" form linked from the audit itself, pre-filled with the audit ID, or by email. No account, no Git, no special format beyond saying which claim and what's wrong.
3. **Challenges are triaged in public.** Each becomes an issue. We respond with one of three outcomes: the audit stands (with the reasoning shown), the audit is amended (erratum appended, dated, with the challenger credited unless they ask otherwise), or the audit is withdrawn (a tombstone note stays — withdrawn audits are part of the record too).
4. **Nothing is silently edited.** The original text stays reachable; changes are a visible, dated layer on top of it. If we ever have to choose between looking consistent and being correct, we choose correct — and show the seam.

## Timeframes we commit to

- **Acknowledgement** of a challenge within **5 working days** of receipt — a tracked issue with an ID.
- **A decision** — audit stands, amended, or withdrawn, with reasoning — within **30 days** of receipt. If a decision genuinely needs longer, we publish a dated note in the issue saying why and when it will land.
- A **minor error** (one that changes no verdict: a date, a non-load-bearing figure, a broken link) is corrected by dated erratum. A **major error** (one that changes, weakens, or removes a verdict, or misidentifies a subject) is corrected at the top of the audit as well as in the errata, and the revised conclusion is labelled as revised.

## Corrections travel

A correction is not made until it has reached the readers of the original. When an audit is amended, the correction is pushed through **every channel the original was distributed in** as far as we control them: the repository record, the audit's StoryTeller presentation and any hosted copy of it, PDFs, and any syndicated version. An erratum appended where the original readers will never see it does not count as a correction.

## If you dispute our decision

Ask for escalation. A challenge that the desk has decided can be reviewed by a **reviewer appointed per case by the desk lead, from outside the team that produced the audit, who was not involved in the audit**. The reviewer is named in the published decision, and the reviewer's identity, the review, and the outcome are recorded on the audit itself. Escalation is requested through the same challenge issue — say "escalate" and it happens in the open.

## What we owe you back

- A receipt: your submission exists as a tracked issue with an ID.
- A verdict on your challenge, with reasoning, not a form letter — within the timeframes above.
- Credit for corrections that change a published audit (or anonymity, if you prefer).
- Honesty about limits: if we can't check something, we say so, in those words.

## What this is not

Feedback here is not a suggestion box that disappears into a roadmap. It is the correction mechanism of a public evidence standard — the same mechanism we ask the subjects we audit to provide for their users, and which Claim Checker exists, in part, because one company didn't.

## Suggesting what to audit next

Subject suggestions go through the "Suggest a subject" form. A subject is any claim-maker — a company, a fund, a public figure, an individual making public claims. Priority goes to subjects whose claims are (a) load-bearing in public — fundraising, hiring, press coverage built on them — and (b) checkable against a public record. Being suggested is not a verdict; every subject gets the same battery, the same contradiction pass, the same right of reply before publication, and the same right to challenge the result.
